# Sourdough Notebook

## Starter

The starter is a 50:50 white and wholemeal rye culture, kept at 100 % hydration.

Feeding when baking every week: discard all but 20 g, then feed 1:5:5 (20 g starter, 100 g flour, 100 g water) the evening before mixing. At 24 °C it peaks in 8–10 hours, roughly doubled, domed and smelling of yoghurt.

Between bakes the starter lives in the fridge. Feed it once a week, and give it two room-temperature feeds before baking again. If a grey liquid ("hooch") forms on top, pour it off; it means the starter is hungry, not dead.

## Basic country loaf (one large loaf)

| Ingredient | Weight | Baker's % |
|---|---|---|
| Strong white flour | 450 g | 90 % |
| Wholemeal flour | 50 g | 10 % |
| Water | 375 g | 75 % |
| Ripe starter | 100 g | 20 % |
| Salt | 10 g | 2 % |

1. Mix flour and 350 g of the water; rest for 45 minutes (autolyse).
2. Add the starter, then the salt with the remaining 25 g of water. Squeeze through until smooth.
3. Bulk ferment at 24 °C for about 5 hours, with four sets of stretch and folds in the first two hours. The dough should grow by about 50 %, be jiggly and show bubbles on the sides.
4. Pre-shape, rest 20 minutes, shape tightly and place seam-up in a floured banneton.
5. Cold retard in the fridge for 12–16 hours.
6. Bake in a preheated Dutch oven at 250 °C, lid on, for 20 minutes; then lid off at 230 °C for 22–25 minutes until deep brown. Internal temperature should be at least 96 °C.
7. Cool for at least two hours before cutting.

## Rye variation

Replace the wholemeal with 100 g dark rye and reduce the white flour to 400 g. Rye absorbs more water but builds less gluten, so keep hydration at 75 % and shorten bulk fermentation to about 4 hours. Add 1 teaspoon of caraway seeds if you like.

## Troubleshooting

**Dense crumb with big holes at the top (flying crust).** Usually underproofed. Extend bulk fermentation until the dough has grown 50 % and feels airy.

**Flat loaf that spreads.** Overproofed, or the shaping was too loose. Shorten the cold retard or build more tension when shaping.

**Pale crust.** The Dutch oven was not hot enough, or the lid stayed on too long. Preheat for a full 45 minutes.

**Very sour taste.** Long cold retards and rye make the bread more sour. For a milder loaf, use a younger starter (6 hours after feeding) and retard for only 8 hours.

**Gummy crumb.** Cut too early, or underbaked. Check the internal temperature and wait the full two hours.
