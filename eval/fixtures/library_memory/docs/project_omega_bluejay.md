# Project Omega — Blue Jay Migration Study, Field Report 2025

## Summary

Project Omega tracks the autumn movements of blue jays (Cyanocitta cristata) along the lake shore ridge. In 2025 volunteers counted 18,420 blue jays passing the ridge watch point between 1 September and 31 October, the second-highest total in the study's nine years. Peak passage was on 26 September, with 2,310 birds in four hours.

## Methods

Counts were made from the ridge platform from sunrise for four hours on every day with suitable weather (no rain, wind under 30 km/h). Two observers counted birds flying south-west past a fixed line between two marker poles. Twelve birds were fitted with 1.2 g GPS tags in late August.

## Findings

- Passage was strongly linked to clear mornings after a cold front: 71 % of the season's birds passed on 14 such days.
- Flocks were larger than in previous years (median 24 birds, against 15 in 2024).
- Of the twelve tagged birds, seven moved more than 150 km south-west, three stayed within 20 km of the ridge all season, and two lost their tags.
- The resident birds that stayed fed heavily on the oak crop, which was unusually large in 2025.

## Discussion

Blue jay migration is partial and irregular: some individuals migrate in one year and stay in another. The large acorn crop may explain why a quarter of the tagged birds stayed. Next season the project will tag 20 birds and add a second watch point 30 km along the ridge.
